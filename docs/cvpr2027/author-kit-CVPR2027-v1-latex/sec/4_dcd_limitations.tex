\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our study has several limitations.  It covers 12 datasets, 18 training
images, and a single SAM ViT-L backbone; other backbones and larger support
sets remain untested, so we do not claim architecture-agnostic generality.  The macro average weights datasets equally, but DCD
is not best on every dataset: for example, it trails specialized or
prompt-based methods on HRF and STARE, and its HD95 is higher on several
datasets, including DeepCrack, STARE, XCAD, and TYRE.  TopoMortar remains a
clear failure case relative to SAM-OCTA.  The
deployed slot is selected on a six-image validation set and then frozen;
this makes deployment deterministic but may be suboptimal for an individual
unseen dataset. Because slot~3 is recall-biased, it can hurt when false
positives or boundary distance dominate; the same datasets above show this
effect. A static slot also cannot adapt per image. The no-fusion variant is a
boundary-oriented alternative: it
improves HD95 from 63.13 to 62.77 at a small overlap cost.  Finally, baselines
use their official implementations and are adapted to the shared 3-shot data
protocol; each keeps its official input resolution and prompt handling, and
SAM-OCTA/SAM-Med2D use oracle prompts.  Published numbers from other codebases
are not directly comparable.  Four seeds reduce split variance but do not
eliminate it.
